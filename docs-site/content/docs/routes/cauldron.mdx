Cauldron is an exchange on Bitcoin Cash: many small pools, each a contract holding BCH and one token. Hashi talks to Cauldron's **Router** (`router.riften.net`) over one WebSocket from your browser.

| Method | What Hashi uses it for |
|---|---|
| `route.quote` | the best path through the pools for a price |
| `tx.build` | the unsigned trade, funded by your own coins or the swap contract's coin, paying your own addresses |

## Fees Hashi adds back

The Router's quotes leave out two costs; Hashi adds both before showing an amount.

| Cost | Rule |
|---|---|
| Router fee | 0.1% of the trade: in BCH for token ⇄ BCH, in the sold token (on top) for PUSD ⇄ MUSD |
| Miner fee | about 1 sat per byte: Hashi counts 260 sats plus 192 per pool (measured: 1 pool 452–500, 22 pools 4,394, 48 pools 9,495 sats) |
| Token output | a bought token output carries 1,000 sats, which stay with you |

## One transaction, signed by you or your swap's key

Each build lists your coins (or the swap contract's coin) as inputs and the pools as the rest. Your Bitcoin Cash wallet signs only your own inputs over WizardConnect; a swap contract's coin is signed with that swap's own key in your browser. Hashi broadcasts the signed trade through Electrum servers. A trade settles at the built price or not at all: it spends the pools' current coins, so if someone else trades those pools first it is no longer valid and the network refuses it.

**PUSD ⇄ MUSD** is one "combined" trade through both tokens' pools. A live, unsigned build on 16 November 2026 sold 10 PUSD for 9.74 MUSD across 54 pools; the seller gave 10.01 PUSD (the fee on top) and the miner fee was 10,901 sats, paid from plain BCH in the wallet.
